Technical Analysis: Ferrari SF-24 vs McLaren MCL38 at Monaco

The 2024 Monaco Grand Prix provided a fascinating technical contrast between the Ferrari SF-24 and the McLaren MCL38.

CAR SETUP PHILOSOPHY
Ferrari opted for a high-downforce, low-drag setup specifically tuned for Monaco's low-speed corners and technical sections. The SF-24's aerodynamic platform proved particularly well-suited to the slow-speed corners of the Monaco layout, particularly Loews Hairpin, Rascasse, and Anthony Noghes.

McLaren's MCL38 is known for its strong performance in medium- and high-speed corners. Monaco's unique layout — with an average speed below 160 km/h — did not play to the MCL38's primary aerodynamic strengths. The car showed some understeer tendencies on the exit of slow corners, which cost Norris time.

TYRE MANAGEMENT
Leclerc demonstrated superior tyre management throughout the race. On the medium compound, Ferrari's smooth, consistent pace minimised thermal degradation. The SF-24's mechanical balance allowed Leclerc to avoid excessive rear tyre wear, a key differentiator at Monaco.

Norris experienced higher-than-expected degradation. McLaren's analysts noted post-race that the MCL38's setup generated slightly more front tyre stress under braking into the tight corners. Monaco's stop-start character exacerbated this, as the repeated heavy-braking zones required consistent front tyre performance across 78 laps.

SAFETY CAR STRATEGY
The safety car on lap 27 was the pivotal moment. Ferrari pitted Leclerc immediately, gaining a free pit stop. McLaren's decision to stay out cost Norris track position. In a circuit where overtaking is virtually impossible, this decision ultimately defined Norris's race result.

SECTOR TIME ANALYSIS
In qualifying, Ferrari held clear advantages in Sector 2 (tunnel, chicane complex) and Sector 3 (Rascasse, Anthony Noghes). McLaren was competitive in Sector 1 but could not replicate Ferrari's pace in the technical middle and final sectors. This reflects Ferrari's advantage in mechanical grip and downforce at very low speeds.

CONCLUSION
The Monaco result was not simply a strategy victory — Ferrari had a genuine car advantage at this specific circuit. The combination of superior downforce configuration, better mechanical grip at low speeds, and outstanding tyre management by Leclerc produced a dominant victory. McLaren will look to circuits that suit their car's characteristics better.
