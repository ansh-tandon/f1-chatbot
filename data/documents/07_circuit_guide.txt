CIRCUIT DE MONACO — TRACK GUIDE

Location: Monte Carlo, Monaco
Circuit length: 3.337 km
Lap record: 1:12.909 (Lewis Hamilton, 2021)
Number of laps (race): 78
Total race distance: 260.286 km
Race direction: Clockwise
DRS zones: 1 (between Nouvelle Chicane and Tabac)

KEY CORNERS AND SECTORS

Sector 1: Sainte Dévote to Massenet
- Sainte Dévote (Turn 1): A right-hand corner taken at medium speed (~100 km/h). Heavy braking required. Key for traction out of the corner.
- Beau Rivage: Long uphill straight building speed.
- Massenet (Turn 5): Fast left-hander leading to Casino Square.
- Casino Square: Right-hand hairpin. Low speed, requires precise positioning.

Sector 2: Mirabeau to Tunnel Exit
- Mirabeau (Turn 8): Medium-speed right-hander.
- Loews/Grand Hotel Hairpin (Turn 9): The slowest corner in Formula 1, taken at approximately 40 km/h. Pure mechanical grip.
- Portier: Medium-speed right-hander before the tunnel.
- Tunnel: The longest straight at Monaco. Cars reach 290+ km/h. One of the best DRS opportunities.
- Nouvelle Chicane: Heavy braking after the tunnel. Critical braking point.

Sector 3: Swimming Pool to Finish
- Swimming Pool (Turns 15-16): Fast chicane requiring precision and commitment.
- Rascasse (Turn 17): Low-speed hairpin. Very tight, crucial for lap time in qualifying.
- Anthony Noghes (Turn 18): Final corner leading onto the start/finish straight.

CIRCUIT CHARACTERISTICS
- Lowest average speed of any F1 circuit (~157 km/h)
- Very narrow track — barriers are extremely close throughout
- Overtaking is nearly impossible once in racing position
- Track position is the defining strategic variable
- Tyre degradation is low due to slow speeds but high due to stop-start braking character
- Mechanical grip more important than aerodynamic downforce
- Qualifying performance is disproportionately important
- Safety car probability is high due to incident likelihood in narrow conditions

TYRE COMPOUNDS AT MONACO 2024
Pirelli selected: C3 (Hard), C4 (Medium), C5 (Soft)
Soft: Used primarily in qualifying. Provides maximum grip but degrades rapidly in race conditions.
Medium: Preferred race starting compound. Good balance of performance and durability.
Hard: Used as the primary second stint compound. Durable for long stints.
